## What drove the big moves

**Maple — syrupUSDC +$343.4M.** syrupUSDC went [live on Arc from day one](https://maple.finance/insights/syrupusdc-is-live-on-arc) — Circle's institutional L1, whose [mainnet opened on Sep 16](https://www.arc.io/blog/arc-mainnet-goes-live-on-september-16-2026), the first day of the window. Two days earlier Maple [joined Zodia Custody's Interchange network](https://x.com/maplefinance/status/2099474670680449329), letting institutions borrow from Maple while their collateral stays in Zodia's custody, and its [September memo](https://maple.finance/insights/maple-memo-september-2026) (Sep 17) reported syrupUSDG launching on Robinhood Chain and Ethereum. *Possible causes:* Arc distribution adding deposits (evidence: timing), and yield — APY rose 4.98% → 5.16% over the week. Neither Maple's blog nor its X posts explain why +$167.7M arrived on one day, Sep 19, and the trend is mixed, so it's too early to call this a new trend.

**Ethena — USDe +$197.4M.** Third straight week of growth (+20.10% over 30d), and it came with APY *falling* (4.99% → 4.65%), so yield doesn't explain it. The clearest driver on record is demand for USDe as collateral: Ethena says it became [Morpho's largest dollar collateral as its Robinhood and Coinbase integrations grew](https://x.com/ethena/status/2097584936295547110) (Sep 9), and that [two of Morpho's three largest markets are now backed by USDe](https://x.com/ethena/status/2102011157129343125) (Sep 21) — consistent with USDe / USDG on Morpho Robinhood adding +$15.3M this week. USDe also [launched on TRON](https://x.com/ethena/status/2098381594293846217) on Sep 11. *Possible cause:* borrowing demand against USDe on Morpho (evidence: Ethena's own statements and the Robinhood market above) — though that tracked market explains only a fraction of the week's growth. Next week: Binance Wallet [added USDe to its Hold to Earn rewards](https://x.com/ethena/status/2102724151920468164) on Sep 23.

**Aave — four flagged items, four different stories.**

- *Ethereum −$279.9M* is one USDT event. Supply fell −$243.7M on Sep 19 with borrowing unchanged, the signature of one large supplier leaving rather than broad demand. No public explanation found. A coverage caveat: Aave's new V4 [passed a record $1B in deposits on Sep 16](https://x.com/aave/status/2100283036281536660) and [went live on Arc](https://x.com/aave/status/2100174774932496778) the same day, while Huma Radar tracks V3 markets only — so money moving from V3 to V4 would show here as an outflow. Nothing public ties the Sep 19 exit to V4. Utilisation at 93.0% has pushed USDT supply APY to 3.77%, a rate that tends to pull deposits back.
- *Plasma +$72.7M* was capacity, not a surge in demand. USDe filled its 550M supply cap within two days, and Risk Stewards [raised it to 750M on Sep 22](https://governance.aave.com/t/risk-stewards-supply-and-borrow-cap-changes-on-aave-v3-2026-09-22/25685), noting 100% cap utilisation and six top suppliers looping at health factors of 1.01–1.05. That's headroom for further inflows, and a concentration worth watching. The same post cut Aave Core's USDe borrow cap from 700M to 100M.
- *Aave USDC −$37.8M is not an outflow.* The row is a lending pool (◇), so it shows available liquidity. Huma Radar's own Aave Ethereum USDC reserve shows supply flat while borrowing rose — liquidity was borrowed out, not withdrawn.
- *Monad −$30.6M* is a three-week decline, spread across USDC, USDT0 and USDe. No public explanation found.
- Context: LlamaRisk [proposed on Sep 9](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/news/aave-proposes-higher-usde-borrow-costs-as-ai-server-goes-live) raising USDe's base borrow rate from 5% to 6% on Core, Plasma, Monad, Mantle and Avalanche. Execution isn't confirmed in what I found.

**Morpho — Robinhood +$52.3M, Base +$29.0M.** Both are three-week uptrends led by Steakhouse vaults, not one-offs. Steakhouse USDG is the vault behind [Robinhood Earn, which runs on Morpho](https://morpho.org/blog/robinhood-chooses-morpho-to-power-new-earn-product) (announced July); this week continues that product's growth, alongside the USDe collateral demand above. On Base, Steakhouse High Yield USDC Edition accounts for the whole move, with its APY rising 2.81% → 3.00% — consistent with deposits following yield. The uptrend predates Morpho's new Base products, [loans against Coinbase tokenized stocks](https://x.com/Morpho/status/2100887060068671988) (Sep 18) and [Coinbase fixed-rate loans](https://morpho.org/blog/coinbase-launches-fixed-rate-crypto-backed-loans-powered-by-morpho-midnight) (Sep 22), so they can at most have added to it late in the week. Separately, Morpho went [live on Arc](https://morpho.org/blog/morpho-is-live-on-arc-as-its-credit-infrastructure) with [$220M+ already deployed through its vaults there](https://x.com/Morpho/status/2100253961806811348) — capital Huma Radar doesn't yet see.

**Fluid — USDC −$24.2M.** A genuine deposit outflow: unlike Aave's row, this one is total supplied, verified against Fluid's own API. APY fell 4.71% → 4.18% the same week. *Possible cause:* yield-driven exits (evidence: the APY drop). Neither the [Fluid blog](https://fluid.io/blog) nor Fluid's X posts in the window mention its USDC market.

**Cap — stcUSD +$18.4M.** A +$25.8M day on Sep 19, partly reversed. Two changes landed just before it: cUSD [moved onto PayPal's PYUSDx platform](https://x.com/CapApp/status/2097697272343789880) on Sep 9, with its reserves now held in PYUSD, and Cap opened a [deposit route from the Fomo app straight into stcUSD](https://x.com/CapApp/status/2099909646266048766) on Sep 15, four days before the jump. *Possible cause:* the Fomo route (evidence: timing only). Cap also reported [about 85% of its USDC deposits lent out](https://x.com/CapApp/status/2098144501026197793) on Sep 10.

**Yuzu — syzUSD +$6.6M.** 82% on Plasma, and +$4.6M of the move came on Sep 22. No public explanation found. No blog or X handle is configured for Yuzu.

*Observation, not a finding:* four of the week's largest single-day moves fell on Sep 19 — Maple +$167.7M, Ethena +$69.8M, Cap +$25.8M, and Aave's USDT −$243.7M. Nothing I found links them.
