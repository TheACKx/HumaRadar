  > Kamino Fixed Rates bring a defined cost of capital and duration to onchain leverage, where financing has historically been dominated by variable-rate, open-term borrowing. This lets borrowers structure leveraged positions around known funding costs rather than utilization-driven rate changes.
  > With Fixed Rate Multiply, a user selects an asset and target leverage, and Kamino builds the position in one transaction.   > How Fixed Rate Multiply Works
  > Fixed Rate Multiply uses Kamino’s existing one-click leverage flow, but the borrow is originated at a fixed rate for a defined term instead of at a variable rate.
  > A user selects an asset and target leverage, and Kamino builds the position in a single transaction:
  > A flash loan provides the capital needed to reach the target leverage
  > The initial deposit and flash-borrowed funds are converted into the collateral asset
  > The full amount is deposited into Kamino as collateral
  > The debt asset is borrowed from a fixed-rate reserve and used to repay the flash loan
  > The result is a leveraged position with a financing cost that is fixed for the full term.
  > If any step fails, the transaction reverts and no position is created. Closing runs the same flow in reverse. The flash loan fee is 0.001% per transaction.
  > What Does a Fixed Rate Change?
  > Fixed Rate Multiply gives leveraged strategies a defined cost of capital over a defined period.
  > Instead of borrowing from a variable-rate reserve, the position borrows from a reserve configured for a specific asset, rate, and term. Once the loan is originated, that rate remains fixed through the end of the term, regardless of changes in utilization elsewhere.
  > Today, AUTO/wYLDS uses three 30-day wYLDS reserves at 5.30%, 5.75%, and 6%. Each reserve has its own available liquidity, and borrowers access that liquidity directly when opening a Fixed Rate position.
  > As Kamino expands Fixed Rates across additional terms and markets, these reserves can form the basis of a broader onchain yield curve.
  > Fixed-term borrowing also introduces different tradeoffs from open-term variable credit. Fixed rates can price at a premium to the prevailing variable rate, and closing during the initial term may incur an Early Repay Penalty to compensate lenders that committed capital for that duration. After the first rollover, positions can be closed without that additional penalty.

  > Auto Rollover: Carrying Positions Across Fixed Terms
  > Fixed-rate credit becomes significantly more useful onchain when borrowers can manage duration without rebuilding positions at every expiry.
  > Three days before the end date, a position enters its rollover window. If eligible fixed-rate liquidity is available, the loan can move into a new term automatically instead of requiring the borrower to close and manually refinance.
  > At expiry, Kamino follows the available path in order:
  > Roll into another eligible fixed term: If qualifying fixed-rate liquidity is available, the position continues into a new term.
  > Move into the variable-rate reserve where supported: If a fixed-rate rollover is unavailable, the debt can fall back to variable borrowing for the same asset in the same market.
  > Auto-repay: If neither path is available, the protocol begins repaying the debt from collateral according to the market’s configured Auto-repay mechanics.
